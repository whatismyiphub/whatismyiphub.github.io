How to Change Your IP Address (and When You Should)

Changing your IP address is easier than most people think, and there are several ways to do it, depending on why you want to. Sometimes the reason is practical: your address is blacklisted, a website has blocked you, or a game server has banned the address you share with others. Sometimes it is about privacy: you would rather not be recognised by an address you have used for months. Whatever the reason, here are the methods that actually work.

The simplest method is to restart your router. Most home connections use dynamic addresses, which means your provider assigns a new address when the router reconnects. Unplug the router, wait thirty seconds, plug it back in, and check your address on our homepage tool. If it changed, you are done. If it did not, leave the router unplugged for several hours or overnight — the longer it is off, the more likely the provider is to give the address to someone else and hand you a fresh one when you return.

The second method is to switch networks. Your phone has a different address on mobile data than on home Wi-Fi, and a cafe or library network gives you yet another. Switching between them changes the address a website sees instantly. It is a quick fix, though it does not change your home address, and it only lasts while you stay on the other network. It is useful when a single site has blocked your home address and you need access in a hurry.

The third method is a VPN. Connect to a VPN server and your traffic appears to come from that server's address. You can choose a server in your own country or another one, and you can change servers whenever you like, getting a new address each time. This is the most flexible option, and it works for all your traffic at once. The trade-off is cost for a good service, and a small loss of speed.

The fourth method is to ask your provider. If your address is static, or if restarting never changes it, your provider can assign you a new one — sometimes for free, sometimes for a small fee. Explain the reason: a blacklist, a block, or a security concern. Providers deal with this regularly and will usually help, though they may need a day or two to process the request.

Before you change your address, it is worth asking whether you need to. If a website blocked you by mistake, changing your address fixes it. If you are worried about being tracked, a VPN helps, but only as part of broader privacy habits. And if your address was blacklisted because a device on your network is infected with malware, changing the address without cleaning the device just moves the problem — the new address will be blacklisted too. Fix the cause first, then change the address, and the fresh start will last.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I change my IP address quickly?

Restart your router. If your address is dynamic, you will usually get a new one when it reconnects.

Does turning on a VPN change my IP?

Yes. Websites see the VPN server's address instead of your real one, and you can change it by switching servers.

Why didn't restarting my router change my IP?

Your provider may hand back the same address. Leave the router off for several hours, or ask your provider for a new address.
