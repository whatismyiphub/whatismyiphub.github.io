What Is a Proxy Server? How It Differs from a VPN

A proxy server is a middleman. When you use one, your requests go to the proxy first, and the proxy forwards them to the website you want. The website sees the proxy's IP address, not yours, and the reply comes back through the proxy to you. It is a simple idea, and it is one of the oldest tricks on the internet — but it is often confused with a VPN, and the difference matters.

The main difference is encryption. A VPN encrypts all of your traffic between your device and the VPN server, so no one on your network can read it. A proxy usually does not encrypt anything: it simply forwards your requests. That means a proxy hides your IP address from the website, but it does not protect your traffic from anyone watching your connection. On an untrusted network, that is an important gap.

Proxies also differ in scope. A VPN protects everything on your device — every app, every browser, every connection. A proxy usually protects one thing: a single browser, or a single app that you configure to use it. That narrow scope can be an advantage. If you only want one browser to appear in another country, a proxy does the job without routing your banking app and your video calls through the same tunnel.

There are several kinds of proxy. A forward proxy is the common kind: it sits between you and the internet, and you choose to use it. A reverse proxy sits in front of a website's servers, distributing traffic and protecting them — the kind that large sites use to stay fast and safe. A transparent proxy is one your network forces you through without telling you, often used by schools and workplaces to filter content. And a residential proxy uses an address from a home internet connection, which makes it harder for websites to detect than a data-centre proxy.

People use proxies for a few practical reasons. The most common is accessing a website as if from another country — checking prices, viewing content, or testing how a site looks abroad. Businesses use them to gather public data from websites without being blocked. Schools and offices use them to filter content and monitor usage. And some people use them as a quick, free way to hide their IP address, though a free public proxy is a risky choice: whoever runs it can see your unencrypted traffic.

The honest comparison is this: a proxy is a simple, narrow tool for changing the address a website sees, while a VPN is a broader tool that also encrypts your traffic. If you need privacy on an untrusted network, use a VPN. If you need one browser to appear in another country for a quick check, a proxy is enough. And if someone offers you a free proxy that seems too good to be true, remember that the operator can read everything you send through it — the price of free is often your data.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a proxy and a VPN?

A VPN encrypts all your device's traffic. A proxy usually only forwards one app's traffic without encryption, hiding your IP address from the website but not protecting the connection itself.

Are free proxies safe?

Often not. A free proxy operator can read your unencrypted traffic. Use a reputable paid service if privacy matters.

When would I use a proxy instead of a VPN?

When you only need one browser or app to appear in another country, and you do not need encryption for the rest of your traffic.
