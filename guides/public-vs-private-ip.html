Public vs Private IP Addresses: What Is the Difference?

Every device on your home network has an IP address, but not all of them are visible to the internet. Your phone might be 192.168.1.5, your laptop 192.168.1.6, and your television 192.168.1.7 — yet when any of them visits a website, the site sees a single public address, like 103.86.12.45. The difference between public and private addresses is one of the most useful things to understand about how a home network works.

A public IP address is globally unique and routable on the internet. It is assigned to your router by your internet provider, and it is the address the outside world uses to reach your home. There is only one public address for your whole household, no matter how many devices you own. A private IP address, by contrast, is only meaningful inside your local network. Your router hands them out to your devices, and they are used for devices to talk to each other — your phone sending a photo to your printer, for example.

Private addresses come from reserved ranges that are never used on the public internet: 192.168.x.x, 10.x.x.x, and 172.16.x.x to 172.31.x.x. These ranges are the same in every home and office in the world, which is why your phone's private address is probably the same as your neighbour's. That is fine, because private addresses are never exposed to the internet. Your router translates between the two worlds using a system called NAT, or Network Address Translation: when a device sends a request out, the router replaces its private address with the public one, and when the reply comes back, the router knows which device it belongs to.

This design has two big benefits. The first is conservation: without NAT, every device in every home would need its own public address, and the world ran out of IPv4 addresses long ago. The second is security: because private addresses are not reachable from the internet, a stranger cannot connect directly to your laptop or your camera. They would have to get through your router first, which is why keeping your router's password strong and its software updated matters so much.

Knowing the difference helps you solve everyday problems. If a support article tells you to check your IP address, it usually means your public one — the one our homepage tool shows. If you are setting up a printer, a media server, or a smart home device, you need its private address, which you can find in your router's admin page or in the device's own network settings. And if a website shows you an address starting with 192.168, it is showing you your private address by mistake — that number is meaningless outside your home.

IPv6 changes this picture slightly, because it has enough addresses for every device to have a public one, and NAT is no longer necessary. But even in IPv6 homes, routers still use firewalls to block unsolicited incoming connections, preserving the same protection. The public-private distinction is a piece of engineering that quietly makes the modern internet possible, letting billions of devices share a limited pool of addresses safely.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is my public IP address?

It is the address your router presents to the internet. Our homepage tool shows it, along with your approximate location and provider.

What is my private IP address?

It is the address your router gives your device on your local network, usually starting with 192.168 or 10. It only works inside your home.

Can someone reach my device using its private IP?

No. Private addresses are not routable on the internet. A stranger would need to get through your router first.
